History of Ukrain independence

Early 20th-Century Fight (1917–1921)

The Ukrainian People's Republic (UPR): Following the fall of the Russian Empire, Ukraine declared its total independence on January 22, 1918, after forming a national parliament known as the Central Rada

The Act of Zluka (1919): The UPR united with the West Ukrainian People's Republic in a symbolic and legal unification of Ukrainian lands.

This early independence movement ultimately fell to Bolshevik forces after years of conflict, leading to Ukraine becoming a founding republic of the Soviet Union (USSR).

The Soviet Era and World War II

Tragedies and Resistance: The decades under Soviet rule brought immense hardships, including the man-made famine known as the Holodomor in the 1930s, and devastating destruction during World War II

As the Soviet Union weakened in the late 1980s, the push for true national sovereignty revived among the public and political figures.

The Declaration: On August 24, 1991, the Verkhovna Rada (the Ukrainian parliament) adopted the Act of Declaration of Independence, establishing a free democratic state.

The Referendum: On December 1, 1991, over 90% of voters—across every single region, including Crimea—approved the independence referendum, sealing the formal end of the Soviet Union

Defending Sovereignty (2014–Present)

Ukrainians took to Maidan Independence Square during the 2004 Orange Revolution and the 2014 Revolution of Dignity (EuroMaidan) to firmly secure their pro-European, democratic path.

Ongoing Resistance: Following Russia's illegal annexation of Crimea in 2014 and the launch of the full-scale invasion on February 24, 2022, Ukraine continues to fight fiercely to protect the freedom and borders it established in 1991
